# Facial Expression Retargeting to 3D Characters

3D · Generative models · ITI diploma thesis · Team of four

## Problem

The goal was to take a person's facial expression and transfer it to a virtual 3D character automatically, for any person, without per-person calibration. Conventional facial animation relies on motion capture or hand keyframing, both of which take a lot of manual work.

## What we built

We implemented the approach from *Facial Expression Retargeting from Human to Avatar Made Easy* (Zhang, Chen & Zheng, 2020):

- **Human VAE.** A graph-convolutional variational autoencoder over 3D face meshes, working on deformation-representation features. Its loss pulls each expression toward the average of that expression across identities, so the latent space captures the expression and not the person.
- **Character VAE.** The same kind of network, one per character.
- **Expression mapping.** Fully connected layers that map the human latent space to the character latent space, trained in two stages:
  1. **Geometric.** Deformation transfer gives an initial character expression, and the network learns to land near it and near the 16 most similar character expressions.
  2. **Perceptual.** Of those 16, a tournament-based selection picks the one closest in perception to the human expression, and the network is refined toward it.

**Data:** the FaceWarehouse dataset (3D meshes of 150 people, 47 expressions each). Character expressions were generated by randomly activating 1–5 rig controllers with random weights, giving 2,000 expressions per character.

## Live input with MediaPipe

To drive the character from video, we used MediaPipe Face Mesh, which estimates 468 3D face landmarks per frame in real time. That's where we hit the main problem: the network was trained on FaceWarehouse meshes with 11,510 vertices in a quad topology, while MediaPipe produces 468 vertices in a triangle topology.

Running the model on quad and on triangulated versions of the meshes made no difference, which pointed at the vertex count. The two fixes we proposed were:

- resample FaceWarehouse to MediaPipe's 468 vertices and retrain, or
- build a new dataset directly with MediaPipe and train on it.

## Result

A working retargeting pipeline on FaceWarehouse meshes, plus a clear diagnosis of what live video input needs. Next steps were retraining on a matching dataset and deploying the model on a server that turns a video stream into an animation file.

## Stack

Graph convolutional networks · variational autoencoders · deformation transfer · MediaPipe Face Mesh · FaceWarehouse
